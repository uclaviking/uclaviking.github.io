Preliminary Overview and Rough Budget for viking.ucla.edu (stage 1)

Summary

viking.ucla.edu will present a media-rich exploration of the world of the Vikings, drawn from the scholarly work of Jesse Byock, professor of Old Norse at UCLA. Although the site will be organized into several topic-specific sections, it will be extensively cross-linked, allowing visitors to follow individual chains of inquiry through the wealth of included materials.

Site Table of Contents

Frequently Asked Questions:
This section will be mostly text based, with some diagrammatic and photographic illustrations (possibly even some video), containing the answers to questions people frequently ask about Vikings, the Scandinavian countries, the Norse languages, and the sagas.

A Brief Introduction to Old Norse and Icelandic:
This section contains a look at the language spoken (and written) during the Viking period, incorporating animated language family trees, an overview of runic writing systems, some basic vocabulary, and audio samples. This can later be expanded to include some interactive lessons from Prof. Byock's Viking Language - Speech and Culture (in preparation).

Law and Government:
This will introduce the basic concepts of the legal systems extant in various Viking and Norse settlements (we will learn what a "thing" is). Charts, diagrams, and timelines will help illustrate these concepts. The role of feud in law and culture will also be covered (and linked to relevant information from the sagas; see below).

Viking, Exploration, and the Settlement of Iceland:
This is where visitors will find what "viking" is, and be able to trace, both geographically and historically, the various viking forays, including the pre-Columbian visits to the "New" World, and the role of the Vikings in the histories of England and Ireland. This section will also present a short history of the settlement of Iceland and its relation to the Vikings.

An Old Norse Atlas:
This will collect a sampling of the maps of Viking activity, taken from Prof. Byock's collection (don't worry, Jesse: this will be an advertisement for the Atlas that you want to publish, not a replacement). Some of the maps will be animated to show movements through time; others will be overlaid with "hot links" that connect to other places on the site (or on other sites) that provide historical, linguistic, or cultural information.

Sagas and Other Tales:
Visitors will find here a description of the sagas, with some translated sections as well as a few pieces in the original Old Norse (possibly even read aloud). A bibliography of modern works that derive from the sagas might also be included.

Sailing the Whale Road (i.e. Navigation and Viking Ships):
In this section, visitors can discover the nautical technologies available to the Vikings, and be able to learn how Vikings built their boats, how they navigated, and what it was like to set sail on such vessels. An exploded view of Viking craft, with hot links providing terminology and historical information will be included.

Archeological Overview:
The major archeological projects and discoveries related to the Vikings will be covered here. There will be links to other material, photographs, diagrams, and, perhaps, a close look at one particular dig so that visitors can get a sense of what the practice of archeology is all about.
