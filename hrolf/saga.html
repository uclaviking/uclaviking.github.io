The Family Sagas

Background

Vapnfirðinga saga belongs to the group of family sagas, or Islendingasögur. Most of the family sagas were composed during the thirteenth and early fourteenth century. The events described in these sagas usually take place during the years 930-1050, also termed as the Saga Age.

The action of the family sagas centers around the following:

  • the settlement of Iceland;
  • the formation of familial clans and political alliances;
  • feuds and lawsuits among families or chieftains - the disputes arising from conflicting claims that involved territory, property, power or social status;
  • the Icelanders' expeditions abroad - their participation in viking raids, their service at the courts of foreign kings and rulers, their exploratory voyages;
  • the Christianization of Iceland;
  • histories of prominent individuals and families.

Literary Style

The family sagas are in many respects unique in comparison with other works of the contemporary medieval literature. They are written in the vernacular, focus on the secular themes and are less obviously imbued with the Christian symbolism than their continental counterparts. Although these sagas were certainly written by Christians, the saga authors display the antiquarian interest in the pagan past rather than condemn the characters on the basis of their erroneous heathen beliefs. They also show a pronounced interest in genealogies and geographical names, providing the reader with extensive lists of the characters' ancestors and relatives and often supplying their own etymologies for place names.

The family sagas also exhibit remarkable precision, economy and clarity of style. The saga writers' narrative distance from the individuals and events they describe and their "realistic" approach to the subject matter of their stories led many critics to characterise the family sagas as remarkable. This impression of impartiality is reinforced by the fact that most of the sagas are anonymous, although attempts have been made to attribute the authorship of some of them to prominent historical figures of the period.

Historical Veracity

It is not wholly clear whether the sagas were considered as reliable historical accounts or fiction by their authors or the audience. It is also unclear whether they originated as oral tales and were merely written down later, or were purely literary works already at the outset.

However, there is ample evidence that many events described in the sagas have their basis in historical facts - such as the political unrest and the civil war in Norway during the reign of King Harald Fairhair, the immigration to Iceland, the discovery of Greenland, the Christianization of Iceland in the year 999-1000, or the establishment of the Law Assembly (Althing) in the year 930 - and that some of the individuals portrayed in the sagas indeed existed. Moreover, regardless of their historical or fictional character the literary merit of the sagas and their contribution into the culture of the contemporary medieval Europe remain undisputed.

Among the most important family sagas are:

  • Brennu-Njals saga
  • Egils saga Skallagrímsonar
  • Laxdæla saga
  • Eyrbyggja sag
  • Grettis saga Asmundarsonar
  • Hrafnkels saga Freysgoda

Manuscript History

Vapnfirðinga saga (The Saga of the People of Vapnafjord) was most likely written in the late thirteenth century. The saga does not survive in its original form: The text was reconstructed from later paper manuscripts, which can be traced back to the same original, and one vellum page. However, there are still some lacunae in the reconstructed version.

Feud in the Saga

As in many other family sagas, the action of Vapnsfirðinga saga revolves almost exclusively around a feud. Two chieftains living in the East Fjords, once fast friends, develop animosities over issues of personal prestige and property - the animosities that eventually escalate, grow into major territorial and power claims and erupt in a violent confrontation. To prevent further outbreaks of violence, their descendants, whose duty it is to pursue the interests of their families and defend their honor, must reach a mutually satisfactory settlement.
